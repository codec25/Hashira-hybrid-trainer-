# HASHIRA Human Movement Foundation

Status: research foundation, not executable programming rules yet.

## Evidence standard
HASHIRA training knowledge should prefer current professional position stands, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, and primary research over influencer routines or gym folklore. Rules must record evidence confidence and must be revisable as evidence changes.

## Research anchors used for this foundation
- ACSM 2026 resistance-training position stand and summary: consistency, major muscle groups at least twice weekly as a broad healthy-adult starting point, goal-specific loading/volume, bodyweight and bands as legitimate resistance modalities, and no requirement for advanced methods or routine training to momentary failure.
- WHO physical-activity guidance: adults generally benefit from 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous aerobic activity weekly plus muscle strengthening on 2+ days; older adults additionally benefit from multicomponent balance/strength work.
- NSCA professional/position guidance: safety, progressive development, force/power development, and appropriate strength-and-conditioning practice.
- Resistance-training meta-analyses: full or long-muscle-length ROM is often useful, training to failure is not universally required, and resistance training itself can improve ROM.
- Pilates reviews: Pilates is its own control-oriented method emphasizing concentration/control/precision/flow/breathing and can improve abdominal endurance and some balance/flexibility outcomes; it must not be represented as merely light resistance training.
- Progressive calisthenics research: changing bodyweight exercise variations can provide progressive resistance and improve strength.

## 1. Universal movement vocabulary
These are movement concepts, not isolated exercises and not mutually exclusive labels.

- squat / knee-dominant lowering and rising
- hinge / hip-dominant flexion-extension
- horizontal push
- vertical push
- horizontal pull
- vertical pull
- lunge / split-stance lower-body work
- carry / loaded locomotion and stabilization
- brace / trunk stiffness and force transfer
- rotation
- anti-rotation
- anti-extension
- anti-lateral-flexion
- reach
- crawl / quadrupedal locomotion
- gait / walking-running family
- jump / takeoff
- land / force acceptance
- balance / postural control
- locomotion / displacement through space

A movement can contain several patterns simultaneously. HASHIRA must not force every exercise into one label.

## 2. Demands are multidimensional
Never use one `difficulty` score as the truth. Track independent demands:

- strength demand
- muscular-endurance demand
- hypertrophy utility
- power demand
- cardiorespiratory demand
- skill complexity
- coordination demand
- balance demand
- mobility requirement
- stability requirement
- impact
- local fatigue
- systemic fatigue
- joint/loading context
- recovery cost
- confidence required for camera-based assessment

This prevents mistakes such as treating a handstand and heavy leg press as equivalent because both are "advanced."

## 3. Programming outcomes
The engine must distinguish at least:

### General health / physical function
Prioritize sustainable exposure to major movement patterns, aerobic activity, strengthening, balance where relevant, and adherence.

### Strength
Favor high force production, sufficient recovery, appropriate specificity, progressive resistance, and technically appropriate exercise selection. ACSM's 2026 healthy-adult summary uses heavier loading around >=80% 1RM as a useful strength-oriented setting for conventional resistance exercise, but HASHIRA must not blindly convert this into bodyweight percentages.

### Hypertrophy
Weekly hard-set volume and meaningful muscular loading matter. ACSM's 2026 summary identifies roughly 10 weekly sets per muscle group as a useful hypertrophy-oriented target, not a universal prescription. HASHIRA must scale volume to experience, recovery, exercise selection, adherence, and the athlete's actual response.

### Power
Power training requires intent to move explosively and appropriate load/skill. High-fatigue work should not routinely precede important speed/power work when quality is the goal.

### Muscular endurance
Use prescriptions that develop repeated force production and local fatigue tolerance without confusing endurance work with maximal-strength work.

### Skill / motor learning
High-coordination skills should usually receive quality practice while sufficiently fresh. Skill progress is not reducible to muscle fatigue or rep count.

### Mobility / flexibility
Distinguish active mobility, passive flexibility, control at range, and sport/task-specific usable ROM. Stretching is one tool, not the entire mobility system. Resistance exercise through useful ROM can itself improve ROM.

## 4. Range of motion
HASHIRA should not teach "deeper is always better" or "partial reps are bad." Evidence supports full ROM for many strength/function outcomes and suggests long-muscle-length work can be useful for hypertrophy, while partial ROM can have valid targeted uses. ROM must be contextualized by the exercise, goal, athlete control, current capability, pain, and discipline.

## 5. Effort and failure
Training to momentary muscular failure is not mandatory for strength or hypertrophy. The engine should support RIR/RPE-style effort targets where appropriate and avoid automatically prescribing failure, especially when technique, power, skill quality, recovery, or safety would suffer.

## 6. Progression is not just "more reps"
Valid progression mechanisms include:

- external load
- leverage
- assistance reduction
- increased usable ROM
- improved control
- longer holds
- tempo manipulation
- greater movement complexity
- unilateral loading
- stability requirement, when purposeful
- density / work capacity
- volume
- frequency when appropriate
- velocity / explosiveness
- better technical execution

The selected progression must match the desired adaptation.

## 7. Regression is not failure
Regressions preserve the training purpose while matching present capability. Examples include assistance, shorter lever, reduced ROM, more stable setup, reduced load, slower complexity progression, or simpler coordination.

## 8. Exercise sequencing
There is no single mandatory session template. As a default reasoning hierarchy:

1. preparation specific to what follows
2. high-skill / high-speed / high-coordination work while fresh when quality matters
3. primary goal work
4. secondary/support work
5. conditioning when compatible with the session goal
6. mobility/cooldown when useful

Discipline packs may override this hierarchy with documented reasons.

## 9. Fatigue and recovery model
HASHIRA should initially reason conservatively from observable/session data rather than pretend to know exact biological recovery.

Track exposure by:
- movement pattern
- muscle region
- exercise family
- session effort
- volume
- load when known
- impact
- skill demand
- user-reported soreness/readiness
- time since exposure
- completion quality

Do not claim a muscle is "83% recovered." Prefer explainable states such as low/moderate/high recent exposure or athlete-reported readiness.

## 10. Pain versus normal training difficulty
Soreness, fatigue, exertion, and pain are not interchangeable. HASHIRA should never diagnose. New/sharp/concerning pain should lead to conservative options: stop the movement, reduce/modify, and seek qualified assessment when appropriate. Camera observations alone must never be labeled as an injury diagnosis.

## 11. Calisthenics interpretation
Calisthenics is resistance training using body mass and leverage, often with substantial skill components. Its progression model must understand:
- leverage changes
- assistance
- relative strength
- static versus dynamic strength
- straight-arm versus bent-arm demands
- range
- body-line control
- skill prerequisites
- external loading as an optional later progression

A progressive push-up study supports the principle that progressively harder bodyweight variations can improve upper-body strength. HASHIRA should therefore model variation difficulty and prerequisites rather than assume bodyweight work is non-progressive.

## 12. Gym / resistance interpretation
The resistance pack must support:
- external load
- machines/free weights/cables/bands
- sets/reps
- RPE/RIR
- rest
- weekly volume
- strength/hypertrophy/power/endurance goals
- progressive overload
- exercise specificity
- fatigue management

It must not overcomplicate training merely to appear intelligent. ACSM's 2026 synthesis specifically reinforces that advanced techniques are optional for the average healthy adult.

## 13. Pilates interpretation
Pilates is modeled as a distinct discipline. Traditional principles include centering/concentration, control, precision, flow, and breathing. Programming should care about trunk organization, alignment, controlled range, coordination, lever length, stability, breathing strategy, and smooth transitions. Progression should increase challenge without sacrificing control simply to chase fatigue.

## 14. Mobility / flexibility interpretation
HASHIRA must distinguish:
- passive ROM
- active ROM
- control at end range
- dynamic mobility
- static stretching
- loaded mobility
- joint/task-specific preparation

A 2025 meta-analysis supports static stretching as a flexibility tool; resistance-training reviews also show meaningful ROM improvements. Therefore the engine should select the tool according to goal rather than assuming every mobility session requires long static stretches.

## 15. First canonical families to model deeply
The first implementation dataset should focus on families rather than hundreds of isolated movements:

1. Squat family
2. Hinge family
3. Push-up / horizontal push family
4. Pike-to-handstand / vertical push family
5. Row / horizontal pull family
6. Pull-up / vertical pull family
7. Dip family
8. Lunge / split-squat family
9. Trunk brace / anti-extension family
10. Rotation / anti-rotation family
11. Hanging / leg-raise family
12. Jump and landing family
13. Carry family
14. Balance family

Each family should later contain entry tests, prerequisites, regressions, standard variations, progressions, prescription options, teaching cues, fatigue metadata, and vision metadata.

## 16. Smart Workout rules derived from this research
The first Smart Workout engine should be deterministic and explainable before it becomes clever:

- choose the session purpose first
- check athlete constraints second
- check recent exposure/readiness third
- choose movement families before individual exercises
- select an exercise variation appropriate to capability
- prescribe dose according to goal and discipline
- sequence for quality and purpose
- ensure regressions are available
- avoid unnecessary failure/extreme methods
- explain the important choices
- collect post-session feedback
- update future recommendations from actual response

## 17. Evidence metadata requirement
Every future programming rule should eventually carry:
- source class (position stand, systematic review, trial, expert consensus, discipline convention)
- evidence confidence
- population studied
- last reviewed date
- whether the rule is universal, goal-specific, or discipline-specific

HASHIRA must be able to revise rules without rewriting the whole exercise library.

## 18. Research gaps / humility
Evidence is stronger for conventional resistance training than for many advanced calisthenics skills, camera-based technique scoring, mixed-discipline programming, and virtual-equipment training. Where evidence is weak, HASHIRA should label the rule as a coaching convention or experimental feature rather than presenting it as settled science.
